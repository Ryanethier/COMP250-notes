# Title: 

The Conflict with Deep Research

# Date: 

September 2nd, 2026

# Source: 

Economist, "Often helpful, sometimes bad, always dangerous" (1 page)

# Valuable Quote:

"At some point, Deep Research, may also be able to come up with amazing ideas, turning it from an assistant to the lead researcher. Until then, use Deep Research, even at $200 a month. Just don't expect it to replace research assistants any time soon. And make sure it doesn't turn you stupid."

# Insight:

This article introduces the concept of bringing Deep Research powered by AI into the economics field. There is a conflict at the heart of this article and its the simple fact that although Deep Research is certainly beneficial for productivity purposes it lacks the ability to have intution and engage with real human discourse (which is an essential part of research). 

I particularly enjoy the bits of humor in this article such as "Nor can it brew a coffee, making it a poor substitute for a human assistant." I believe that we should embrace this humor and viewpoint towards automation whether it be in economics or not. While I'm not anti AI, I feel we should be compelled to call out the flaws to these systems and always place ourselves in a position of power or authority over AI.

To that point, the article ends with the declaration that we shouldn't let this tech (Deep Research) make us stupid. 

It's clear that AI and Deep Research improve productivity and efficency but at what cost, what do we sacrifice as humans when we offshore our intelligence to an artifical technology.
